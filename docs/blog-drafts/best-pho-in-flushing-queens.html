What Makes a Great Bowl of Pho?

Pho looks simple, but every element is doing work.

It starts with the broth — warm, aromatic, and poured over soft rice noodles. Your choice of protein adds the heartiness. Then the plate of garnishes on the side is where the bowl becomes yours: bean sprouts for crunch, Thai basil for fragrance, a squeeze of lime for brightness, jalapeños when you want a kick.

That flexibility is why pho works for almost any mood. The same bowl can read light and refreshing or rich and filling depending on how you build it.

The Five Phở Bowls at Nón Lá Express

Rare Eye Round Beef Pho — best for first-timers

Paper-thin slices of rare eye round, gently cooked by a rich, slow-simmered beef broth poured over rice noodles. This is the classic Vietnamese beef noodle soup and the bowl to order if you've never had pho before — it's the clearest expression of what pho is supposed to taste like, without anything unfamiliar in the bowl.

Fresh Phoenix Chicken Pho — best for a lighter bowl

Tender poached chicken over silky rice noodles in a clear, aromatic broth. All the comfort of pho with a cleaner, more balanced flavor. A good pick for a midday bowl when you don't want to feel weighed down afterward.

Phở Special — best for the full traditional experience

Rare sliced beef, well-done brisket, beef flank, tendon, tripe and beef meatballs in a rich traditional broth. This is the bowl that pho regulars order: every spoonful hits a different texture, and the tendon and tripe are what separate a real Vietnamese beef noodle soup from a simplified version. If you already know you like pho, order this one.

Pho & Grilled — best for smoky flavor

A traditional bowl of pho topped straight off the grill with your choice of pork, chicken or ribeye. The char on the grilled meat plays against the clean broth, which makes this the most satisfying option when you're properly hungry.

Spicy Pho — best for heat

Your choice of chicken or eye round over rice noodles in a chili-forward broth that brings the heat. Bold and warming rather than punishingly hot — the spice is layered into the broth instead of dumped on top.
