What Makes a Great Vietnamese Rice Plate?

A good rice plate is all about balance. The meat brings the richness — charred from the grill or seared hot in a wok. The rice soaks up the juices. Meanwhile, the pickled vegetables cut through the richness with something sharp and crunchy, and the fresh herbs keep every bite bright.

Then there's nuoc cham, the sweet, sour and salty dipping sauce that sits at the center of Vietnamese cooking. Spoon it over the rice or dip each bite — either way, it's what turns grilled meat and rice into a complete dish.

That combination is why rice plates are an everyday Vietnamese meal. They're hearty enough for dinner, quick enough for lunch, and they travel well if you're taking food home.

The Five Vietnamese Rice Dishes at Nón Lá Express

Shaking Beef Rice (Bò Lúc Lắc) — best for bold, savory flavor

Shaking beef gets its name from the way the cubes of beef are tossed — "shaken" — in a very hot wok so every side sears. Ours is wok-seared beef tossed with garlic, onions and peppers, served over rice. It's savory, garlicky and deeply satisfying, which makes bò lúc lắc the plate to order when you're properly hungry.

Lemongrass Beef Rice (Bò Nướng Sả) — best for a special meal

If you've been looking for lemongrass beef in Flushing, this is the one. We marinate ribeye in fragrant lemongrass, then grill it so the edges caramelize. The result is tender, smoky and aromatic. As a result, it's the most indulgent plate on the menu — a good choice when lunch is a treat rather than a refuel.

Pork Chop Rice (Cơm Sườn Nướng) — best for a classic

Grilled pork chop over rice is one of the most familiar Vietnamese rice plates there is. Our pork chop is marinated in lemongrass and grilled, so it comes to the table juicy, slightly sweet and charred at the edges. If you're new to Vietnamese pork chop rice, start here — it's simple, generous and easy to love.

Smoked Paprika Chicken Rice (Cơm Gà Paprika) — best for something different

This is our own twist on a grilled chicken rice plate. The chicken is grilled and glazed with smoked paprika, which gives it a warm, smoky depth. It's a lighter option than the beef plates, but it still has plenty of flavor — especially with a drizzle of nuoc cham over the rice.

Rice Vermicelli Bowl (Bún Thịt Nướng) — best for a lighter, cooler meal

Not every Vietnamese rice bowl is built on steamed rice. Bún thịt nướng swaps it for cool rice vermicelli noodles, layered with fresh herbs and pickles. You choose the topping: spring rolls, pork, chicken or ribeye. It's fresh, textured and refreshing, so it's a great pick on a warm day or whenever you want something that won't weigh you down.

Rice Plates or Pho: Which Should You Order?

Both are Vietnamese staples, but they answer different cravings. Pho is a noodle soup — warm, aromatic and comforting, and the broth is the star. A rice plate, by contrast, is all about the grilled or seared meat, with rice and pickles to balance it.

Vietnamese Rice Plates in Flushing: Common Questions

What is a Vietnamese rice plate?

A Vietnamese rice plate is grilled or wok-seared meat served over rice with pickled vegetables, fresh herbs and nuoc cham. At Nón Lá Express, every rice plate comes with all four.

What is shaking beef?

Shaking beef, or bò lúc lắc, is cubed beef tossed in a very hot wok so it sears on every side. Ours is wok-seared with garlic, onions and peppers and served over rice.

Which rice plate should I order if I've never had Vietnamese food?

Start with the Pork Chop Rice. A lemongrass-marinated, grilled pork chop over rice is familiar, generous and a great introduction to Vietnamese flavors.

What's the difference between bún thịt nướng and a rice plate?

A rice plate is built on steamed white rice. Bún thịt nướng uses cool rice vermicelli noodles instead, with herbs and pickles, so it eats lighter and fresher.
